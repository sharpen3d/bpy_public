A little history

How I got here

From stop-motion animation to digital worlds, games, and the tools behind them. A look back at some of the work that got me here.

Where it started

One frame at a time

I was making stop-motion animations and starting to learn Blender when I was a kid. There was something about making things move one frame at a time that stuck with me.

Around the same time, I was trying to make games in the Blender Game Engine. I didn’t really think of it as wanting to work in games; I was interested in art and animation, and making things move was what I liked. That interest followed me into digital animation and eventually to Bradley University, where I studied visual effects and animation. I started exploring modeling, lighting, materials, and building a whole place for a story to happen.

Animation & images · 2015

Making images move

In college I was experimenting with animation, compositing, and the strange things that happen when you push an image around. Skygazing came out of that period, alongside datamoshing and other visual tests.

2015 · Building environments

Making places, piece by piece

I started building environments as sets of pieces: a modular classroom, a hotel room in Blender, and scenes that could move into Unreal. A lot of the work was figuring out what happened between the programs and why something looked different when it got there.

Materials & systems

Why does it work that way?

The surfaces became their own experiments. I was making materials I could adjust across different objects, vertex-painting wear onto chairs, and finding ways to see changes in real time.

I was also exploring motion in materials. This early animated moss test used Quixel Megascans and Blender Cycles.

College · 2016–2017

Learning to build it all

At this point I was focused on animation and 3D modeling, trying to find a style and learning a little bit of everything along the way. I was modeling modular pieces and variations before I had much sense of how I might use those ideas in a game.

For my senior project I could choose the software, so after working in Cinema 4D and Maya in college, I went back to Blender. It was a solo project and a chance to bring everything I’d been learning together.

2018 · Real-time art

Getting more out of the pixels

I was also learning Substance Designer and Painter and using those materials on real-time assets. I liked being able to get close to a surface and still see all the small details that made it feel used.

For this large robot study, inspired by Simon Stålenhag’s Tales from the Loop, I modeled and textured the subject for Unreal. The interesting problem was keeping a consistent level of texture detail across something huge without relying on enormous image maps.

2018–2020 · Independent work

Putting the pieces together

Over time, these experiments became independent projects, environments, and real-time work. The 2020 reel brings together modeling, textures, environments, and gameplay work from that period.

From making things to making tools

Finding ways around the work

I came into this from the art side and tried to do a lot myself. That naturally made me a generalist: the more I needed to make, the more I needed to understand the software underneath it.

Over time I learned more code, became the person people came to with Blender questions, and then the person they asked to make tools. This early texturing toolset was part of the work that eventually led to Aether: building something for Blender, then using it on small projects to find what needed to change.

Making tutorials became another way to share what I was figuring out. Alongside the tools, I kept getting deeper into game programming and the systems underneath the things I wanted to build.

Still figuring things out

Making the tools, too

These days my work spans technical art, tools, 3D, and game development. I’m proud of how much ground I’ve covered, and I want to keep growing into the technical direction and engineering side of the work. I still like making things, taking them apart, and figuring out how they work.
